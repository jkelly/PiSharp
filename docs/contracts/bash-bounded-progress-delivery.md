# Bash progress delivery under bounded source-compatible observation

Bash explicitly paces its producer with `ToolProgressDelivery.ReportAndWaitAsync`. Both its initial empty update and every actual `IProcessRunner` snapshot await the owned delivery receipt. Under `SourceCompatible` this waits for the actual primary sink and ordered subscribers, including a serialized RPC writer's complete write/flush. An ordinary source-facing callback still returns immediately. The public Agent default, other tools' callback behavior, and every configured quota remain unchanged.

This repairs the B1 composition regression on immutable candidate `e415812de0a745be9d283b9ed486326abfe5b2b9`. Previously, Bash awaited only the source callback's immediate admission return. Its bounded per-chunk tail snapshots could accumulate behind a slow RPC writer, exceeding the existing 8-MiB outstanding-character budget despite each snapshot being admissible. The resulting failure replaced a successful command's final result. The correction changes producer pacing; it does not increase a quota, coalesce reports, skip intermediate frames, substitute a final frame for progress, or suppress the original failure evidence.

The owned prepared-adapter scope admits each logical update once. Its nested scheduler forwarding shares the actual receipt without charging the same update again. The pending-report and retained-character charges release after delivery settles. Bash holds at most one unfinished report of its own; the process backend retains its separately bounded pipe queue and capture state. Parallel invocations and other tools still share the batch-wide budget, total report cap, and pending-report cap. Producer pacing is not a claim of unbounded progress, immunity to unrelated quota consumption, or a total heap limit.

Once a report is admitted, cancellation does not detach its listener or replace its receipt with a canceled wait proxy. Bash joins that receipt and the runner's actual owned cleanup. The real work token remains available to cooperative observers and the runner. A failed listener propagates through the owned receipt and shutdown path; later ordinary snapshots cannot overtake it. Existing process termination, decoder flush, raw spill, final output, structured output, policy, and durable completion contracts remain in force. Cancellation does not undo completed shell effects.

Pinned Pi v0.99.1 emits the initial update and throttles subsequent Bash updates using wall-clock timers; its callback does not await observer completion. Native Bash continues to report each admitted runner chunk and decoder change. Explicit receipt pacing is native bounded-delivery behavior, rather than a claim of identical source callback timing or throttling. Complete result/progress differential and broader shell/platform rows remain required. No source fixture or golden is rewritten for this correction.

`BashProgressDeliveryTests.Cases()` adds three authored native groups:

- A real source-compatible scheduler and prepared invoker hold the initial receipt before runner acquisition, then hold the first large snapshot. The fake runner cannot admit the next snapshot until delivery settles. It reports 120 complete ordered snapshots whose aggregate charge exceeds the unchanged 8-MiB quota, then acknowledges an exact owned raw spill and successful model/details/structured final result.
- Cancellation during held publication cannot return or enter runner cleanup early. Releasing publication enters a separate held cleanup barrier; settlement retains known NUL/Unicode output and has no fabricated normal structured completion.
- A listener fault stops later snapshots, preserves the original observer failure, awaits the runner cleanup barrier, and emits no normal successful tool end or fabricated complete spill.

These tests exercise delivery ownership through the production scheduler/invoker/adapter; their runner is an authored deterministic fake. Existing real Bash, compiled RPC, durable reopen, policy, raw-output and abort fixtures remain unchanged and supply the separate actual-process integration gates. Authoring does not establish a passing build or independent acceptance. Root owns registration, execution and immutable evidence.
